Quest: Survival in the Black Market (1942–1945)

Occupation currency · shortages · barter

During the Japanese occupation, shortages and inflation reshaped daily life in the Philippines. As occupation-issued currency lost value and ordinary supplies became scarce, many people relied on informal markets, bartering, and community networks to find food and other necessities.

Key Events

  1. 1942 onward

    Occupation Currency

    Japanese occupation authorities issue paper pesos. As more notes circulate and confidence falls, the money becomes known as “Mickey Mouse money” and rapidly loses purchasing power.

  2. 1942–1945

    Shortages and Rising Prices

    Wartime disruption and scarcity make food, medicine, fuel, and other everyday goods increasingly difficult to obtain through official channels.

  3. Throughout the occupation

    Barter and Black Markets

    Families trade rice, clothing, tools, and other necessities outside regulated markets. Bartering helps people obtain essentials when money cannot keep up with prices.

  4. 1942–1945

    Networks of Survival

    Neighbors, farmers, and local traders share information and supplies between communities, forming informal networks that help people endure the occupation.
